PART A — FOUNDATIONS
1. Executive Summary
Project APEX is a complete, zero-budget quantitative trading system that you will build and operate alone. Its purpose is not to “find a winning strategy” — it is to build a factory that continuously discovers, validates, filters, sizes, monitors, and retires strategies, the same loop every professional quant firm runs, rebuilt from free open-source components.
The system rests on five pillars:
A data lake of free, institutional-quality crypto market data (Binance public archive: tick-to-daily history since 2017, unlimited downloads, no account required), stored locally in Parquet + DuckDB.
A strategy miner that automatically discovers candidate strategies — first by brute-force combinatorial search over indicator/filter/exit building blocks, later by genetic programming that evolves strategy logic itself, and finally by local machine learning (no paid AI APIs anywhere).
A trade database (“the edge mine”) in which every trade from every backtest and every live session is stored with ~30 labels (strategy, parameters, pair, session, hour, day, volatility regime, trend regime, exit reason, MFE/MAE…). This database is the system’s most valuable permanent asset: it lets you mine when and where each strategy works — your original edge idea, implemented with statistical rigor.
A validation lab that kills weak strategies before they cost money: walk-forward analysis, four kinds of Monte Carlo simulation, Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO), Deflated Sharpe Ratio, and regime stress tests. Expect to reject 95% of what the miner finds. That rejection rate is the system working correctly.
A risk-first portfolio engine and command center: volatility-targeted position sizing, HRP/CVaR allocation across strategies, hard circuit breakers, and a Streamlit dashboard that hands you a clear “edge card” each session so manual execution becomes checklist execution.
What success looks like (12–18 months): a system holding 100,000+ labeled trades; 5–10 validated strategies with out-of-sample-confirmed session/day/regime filters; a portfolio-level risk engine with circuit breakers you actually obey; a live track record of 100+ real trades whose expectancy matches the backtest within tolerance; and a monthly re-mining loop that keeps the edge fresh as markets evolve.
Build time to first validated paper trade: ~12 weeks. To first live trade: ~5–6 months. Budget: ₹0 / $0.

3. Honest Constraints & Realistic Expectations
This chapter exists so that month-6-you doesn’t quit out of misplaced expectations. Read it monthly.
3.1 You cannot beat institutions at their game. Funds have colocated servers, tick-level order flow, teams of PhDs, and billion-dollar data budgets. You will not out-speed or out-data them.
3.2 You can win the game they refuse to play. Institutional capital cannot bother with edges worth 0.3–1% per week on $5,000 — it’s meaningless to them and often too small or capacity-limited to trade. Retail-sized, patient, well-filtered edges in liquid-but-slow timeframes are exactly the niche this system targets.
3.3 Filtering turns break-even into profitable — it does not resurrect garbage. Day/hour/session/regime filtering (your core idea) reliably improves mediocre strategies; it rarely saves a strategy with no underlying edge. The miner’s job is to find raw material; the filter’s job is to refine it.
3.4 Most discovered “edges” are noise. If you test 100,000 variants, thousands look brilliant by pure chance. This is the multiple-comparisons problem, and it is why Part C’s Validation Lab (PBO, Deflated Sharpe, walk-forward) is non-negotiable. The uncomfortable truth: the better the backtest looks, the more skeptical you must be.
3.5 Edges decay. Market regimes shift; other participants arbitrage patterns away. Assume every edge has a half-life of 6–24 months. The monthly re-mining and edge-decay detector exist for this reason.
3.6 The operator is the biggest risk. Manual execution, revenge trading after losses, overriding the system “just this once,” stopping during drawdowns — operator behavior kills more retail systems than bad math. Chapter 29 is as important as any code.
3.7 Expectancy arithmetic (memorize):
Expectancy = (win_rate × avg_win) − (loss_rate × avg_loss) − costs
A strategy is tradeable only if Expectancy > 0 AFTER realistic
fees + slippage + spread, AND n ≥ 100 OOS trades, AND it survives
the Validation Lab.
3.8 Timeline honesty. Month 1–3: building, zero trading income. Month 4–6: paper trading, zero income. Month 6–12: small live trading, modest income if everything works. Compounding is slow at first and then not. Anyone promising faster is selling something.

5. The Twelve Core Design Principles
These principles govern every build decision. When in doubt, the principle that says “be more conservative” wins.
The database is the product. Strategies are disposable; the labeled trade database compounds in value forever. Never run a backtest whose trades aren’t logged.
Costs first, always. Every backtest models fees, spread, and slippage from day one. A backtest without costs is fiction.
In-sample is for searching, out-of-sample is for deciding. The miner never sees the test period. No exceptions, ever.
Sample size or it didn’t happen. No slice, filter, or strategy is trusted below n=100 trades (50 = provisional only).
Simple beats clever. A robust simple strategy beats a fragile complex one. Complexity must earn its place with OOS performance.
Plateaus, not peaks. Choose parameter regions where neighbors also work; a lone peak is an overfit artifact.
Kill ruthlessly. Rejecting 90–95% of candidates is the sign of a healthy pipeline, not a failure.
Everything is reproducible. Every run is config-driven, seeded, logged, and re-runnable byte-for-byte.
Local-first, zero-cost. If a component needs a paid API, it doesn’t belong in the core system.
The system advises; discipline executes. Until auto-execution is earned, the human executes the checklist — not impulses.
Risk is a budget, not an afterthought. Position size, exposure, and circuit breakers are computed by the Risk Engine, never by mood.
Document as you build. A module without a completed QA checklist and a doc page is not “done.”

6.1 Data flow (the daily life of the system)
Nightly (02:00): Data Lake pulls the previous day’s candles for all pairs → validates → appends to Parquet → rebuilds DuckDB views.
Nightly (02:30): Feature Factory recomputes indicators/regimes for the new bars.
On demand / weekly: Miner launches search runs over the in-sample period; survivors are handed to the Backtest Engine for realistic replay.
Every run: all trades → Trade Database.
Nightly (03:00): Edge Analytics recomputes slice statistics; Edge-Decay detector compares live vs historical expectancy per strategy.
Each session (before you trade): Command Center shows today’s Edge Cards + risk budget; Telegram alert fires when a valid setup window opens.
You execute (manually at first); the fill is journaled into the Trade Database with source='live'.
Monthly: Walk-forward re-validation on all data including new live trades; Portfolio Engine rebalances strategy weights; miner re-runs on refreshed data.
6.2 The one-way research wall (critical architecture rule)
|←——— IN-SAMPLE (2017–2022): miner + analytics may touch ———→|‖|←— OUT-OF-SAMPLE (2023→now): validation only —→|
Code enforcement: the Data Lake exposes get_data(start, end); the Miner process is only ever passed end='2022-12-31'. The OOS segment is loaded only inside the Validation Lab. This is enforced by configuration, not by memory.

9. Module 1 — Data Lake
9.1 Purpose
Provide the entire system with clean, complete, validated, fast local market data. Every downstream module trusts the Data Lake blindly — therefore the Data Lake must earn that trust with aggressive validation.
9.2 Responsibilities
Bulk-download historical klines (candles) from Binance public archive for the configured universe
Incrementally update data nightly via CCXT
Resample base granularity into all higher timeframes deterministically
Validate data quality (gaps, duplicates, outliers, zero-volume bars, clock integrity)
Maintain auxiliary datasets: funding rates, mark price (for perp-aware backtests), and a macro event calendar
Serve data through one function: get_bars(pair, timeframe, start, end) -> DataFrame
9.3 Inputs / Outputs
9.4 Schema — canonical bar
open_time  TIMESTAMP UTC (bar open, Binance convention, ms→ts)
open       DOUBLE
high       DOUBLE
low        DOUBLE
close      DOUBLE
volume     DOUBLE          (base asset)
quote_vol  DOUBLE          (quote asset)
trades     INTEGER         (count of trades in bar)
taker_buy  DOUBLE          (taker buy base volume)
pair       VARCHAR
timeframe  VARCHAR
All downstream code uses exactly these column names. Resampling rule: right-label nothing; bars are indexed by open time; aggregation uses first/max/min/last/sum for O/H/L/C/V and sum for trades, quote_vol, taker_buy.
9.5 Implementation steps
Bulk downloader (src/datalake/binance_archive.py): for each pair & month from listing date to now, request https://data.binance.vision/data/spot/monthly/klines/<PAIR>/<TF>/<PAIR>-<TF>-<YYYY>-<MM>.zip; stream, unzip in memory, append to Parquet. Use the 1m klines as the base layer; resample locally to 5m/15m/1h/4h/1d (never download every timeframe — the archive’s higher TFs are redundant bytes). Concurrency: 4–8 threads, checksum verify, resume-safe (skip months already stored).
Incremental updater (src/datalake/updater.py): nightly APScheduler job → CCXT fetch_ohlcv from last stored timestamp → append → validate.
Validator (src/datalake/validate.py): runs after every write; checks listed in §9.7.
Resampler (src/datalake/resample.py): vectorized pandas resample; writes per-TF Parquet; unit-tested against hand-computed examples.
Access layer (src/datalake/api.py): the ONLY import the rest of the system uses:
def get_bars(pair: str, tf: str, start: str, end: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """UTC-indexed OHLCV+ DataFrame from Parquet via DuckDB pushdown."""
Event calendar (data/calendar/): crypto-relevant events — FOMC/CPI dates (macro risk-off), token unlocks for held alts, Binance listing/delisting notices. Start with a manually maintained CSV; automate later.

10. Module 2 — Feature & Regime Factory
10.1 Purpose
Transform raw bars into the labeled, feature-enriched dataset that the miner, backtester, and edge analytics all consume. Regime labels computed here are the dimensions along which your edge filtering happens.
10.2 Feature groups
*Crypto has no official sessions, but global liquidity still follows the sun: Asia/Europe/US windows are empirically meaningful and give you the same session-edge analysis as forex traders get.
10.3 Regime definitions (pinned, version-controlled)
regimes:
  trend:
    up:    "ema200_slope_20bar > 0 AND adx14 >= 22"
    down:  "ema200_slope_20bar < 0 AND adx14 >= 22"
    range: "adx14 < 22"
  volatility:
    low:     "atr_pctile_252d < 0.25"
    mid:     "0.25 <= atr_pctile_252d < 0.75"
    high:    "0.75 <= atr_pctile_252d < 0.95"
    extreme: "atr_pctile_252d >= 0.95"
10.4 Implementation rules
Point-in-time correctness: feature at bar t may only use data ≤ t (no lookahead). Every custom feature gets a lookahead unit test: shift the series by one bar, confirm feature values shift identically.
Compute once, store, reuse: features are materialized to data/features/<pair>/<tf>.features.parquet, rebuilt nightly and versioned (feature_version column) when definitions change.
No NaN leakage: warm-up rows (first ~252 bars) are marked, not silently dropped mid-pipeline; strategies decide whether to trade warm-up regions.
Deterministic: same input → same output; no randomness anywhere in this module.

11. Module 3 — Strategy Miner
11.1 Purpose
Automate the discovery of candidate strategies. Humans are slow and biased hypothesis generators; the miner explores the combinatorial space systematically and hands the Validation Lab a ranked survivor list.

11.3 Miner-1: Brute-force combinatorial miner (build FIRST)
Building blocks (each a versioned function returning a boolean series):
Search strategy — staged, not full-cartesian (full cartesian of the above is ~10⁸; too much):
Stage 1 — solo scan: every entry trigger alone × timeframes × pairs (~30 × 4 × 20 = 2,400 runs). Establish baseline expectancy of each trigger.
Stage 2 — pair-up: top 30% triggers × every filter × fixed exit (~10 × 20 × 12 × 4 × 20 ≈ 192,000 runs — vectorbt handles this overnight).
Stage 3 — exit search: top 500 combos × all exit models × exit params.
Stage 4 — plateau analysis: for each survivor, perturb each parameter ±20%; score = fraction of neighbors still profitable (plateau score ≥ 0.6 required).
Ranking score (in-sample, pre-validation):
score = expectancy_bps × log10(n_trades) × plateau_score × consistency
where consistency = fraction of profitable years in-sample
11.4 Miner-2: Genetic programming miner (Phase 9)
Engine: DEAP (genetic algorithm framework) + gplearn-style expression trees.
Genome: expression tree over feature primitives, e.g. AND(GT(RSI(14), 68), LT(close, MUL(VWAP, 0.98))).
Fitness: OOS-segment-1 Sharpe × sqrt(n) − λ×tree_depth — penalize complexity explicitly (λ in config).
Anti-overfit mechanics: (a) fitness evaluated on in-sample sub-segment A, selection pressure from sub-segment B (GeneTrader-style “selection bar”: the distribution of best-of-N luck); (b) max tree depth 6; (c) island model (separate populations, occasional migration) for diversity; (d) final candidates must pass the full Validation Lab like everything else — genetic search is a hypothesis generator, not a shortcut around validation.
Compute budget: overnight runs (~8h); population 500 × 40 generations.
11.5 Miner-3: Local ML layer (Phase 10+, free)
Task framing: predict P(trade profitable | features at entry) using the Trade Database itself as labeled training data — the system literally learns from its own edge mine.
Models: gradient boosting (XGBoost/LightGBM) + logistic regression baseline. SHAP for interpretability (which features drive success — often reveals unknown session/regime structure).
Validation: purged, embargoed time-series CV (López de Prado method) to prevent leakage; predictions only ever trade-sized through the Risk Engine, never naked.
Hard rule: ML output is a filter/size adjuster on top of validated strategies, never a standalone black-box strategy, until you have 12+ months of live evidence.
11.6 Mining governance (what makes this professional)
Every mining run is registered in runs/ with: config hash, git commit, date range, variant count, machine, duration.
Trial budget accounting: the miner logs n_variants_tested — this number feeds the Deflated Sharpe calculation. You cannot compute honest significance without knowing how many things you tried.
Survivors are candidates, never strategies. Only the Validation Lab can promote a candidate.
Miner never sees post-2022 data. Enforced at the data-access layer.

14. Module 6 — Edge Analytics Engine
14.1 Purpose
Answer, with statistical honesty: “When, where, and under what conditions does each strategy actually make money?” — and package the answer as actionable Edge Cards.
14.2 Core capabilities
Slice statistics: arbitrary filter → n, expectancy (R & bps), win rate, profit factor, Sharpe-like ratio, avg win/loss, max consecutive losses, equity curve of slice.
Dimension scans: auto-loop over (hour × day), (session × regime), (month), (event proximity) producing heatmap tables.
Significance layer (the professional part):
t-test / bootstrap CI on mean R per slice
Multiple-testing correction: Bonferroni (conservative) or Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across all slices tested in a scan
Stability requirement: slice must be profitable in BOTH halves of in-sample (2017–2019, 2020–2022)
Minimum n: 100 (50–99 = “provisional”, never traded live)
Edge Card generator: converts a validated slice into the operational card:
EDGE CARD #41 — momo_breakout / SOLUSDT / 15m
TRADE ONLY: session ∈ {europe, us-overlap} AND day ∈ {Tue,Wed,Thu}
            AND trend_regime = up AND vol_regime ∈ {mid, high}
SKIP: event days, vol_regime = extreme
STATS: n=412 | exp=+0.31R | WR=47% | PF=1.52 | p<0.01 (BH-adj) | stable both halves
OOS (2023-25): n=96 | exp=+0.24R ✓ confirmed
14.3 Anti-self-deception protocol (built into the code, not willpower)
Every scan reports how many slices were tested and the adjusted threshold alongside.
Every Edge Card carries its p-value, n, and both-half stability — cards missing any field cannot be marked active.
Slices discovered by the miner are re-tested on OOS before activation; post-hoc slices (you noticed a pattern) require a fresh holdout or next-3-months forward test before activation.
14.4 Implementation
src/edge/: - slice.py — filter → stats dict - scan.py — dimension iterators → results DataFrame → Parquet cache - significance.py — tests, corrections, stability splits - cards.py — Edge Card CRUD + expiry (cards auto-expire 90 days after last validation) - UI exposure in Command Center page “Edge Explorer”

15. Module 7 — Validation Lab (Anti-Overfitting Suite)
15.1 Purpose
The immune system of the whole operation. Its only job: reject. A strategy that passes everything here has earned the right to be paper traded; nothing reaches your money without this stamp.
15.2 The gauntlet (in order — cheap tests first, so weak strategies die fast)
Candidate
  │
  ├─ GATE 1: OOS replay (2023→now, unseen)      expectancy > 0, PF > 1.1
  ├─ GATE 2: Walk-forward analysis               WFE ≥ 0.5, no catastrophic fold
  ├─ GATE 3: Monte Carlo ×4                      DD CI acceptable, skip-robust, cost-robust
  ├─ GATE 4: Regime stress                       survives all 6 regime cells
  ├─ GATE 5: PBO (CSCV)                          PBO < 0.20
  ├─ GATE 6: Deflated Sharpe                     DSR p-value < 0.05 given n_variants tried
  └─ VERDICT: VALIDATED / KILLED (with written reason)
15.3 Gate specifications
Gate 2 — Anchored walk-forward: - Optimize on [2017–2020] → test [2021]; roll: optimize [2018–2021] → test [2022]… through OOS years; 6+ folds. - Metric: Walk-Forward Efficiency = annualized OOS return / annualized IS return. WFE ≥ 0.5 required; also no fold with DD > 1.5× IS max DD. - Output: stitched OOS equity curve — the only honest equity curve the strategy has.
Gate 3 — Monte Carlo battery (all four, 10,000 sims each):
Gate 4 — Regime stress: strategy P&L computed per cell of {trend-up, trend-down, range} × {low-vol, high-vol}; requirement: no cell with expectancy < −0.3R AND n ≥ 30 (losing mildly in one regime is fine — filters may fix it; catastrophic single-regime dependence is not).
Gate 5 — PBO (Combinatorially Symmetric Cross-Validation, López de Prado): - Split in-sample equity matrix (all parameter variants × time) into 16 blocks; form all C(16,8) train/test combos (12,870). - For each combo: rank variants on train, check where the chosen-best ranks on test (logit of rank). - PBO = fraction of combos where the train-best underperforms test median. PBO < 0.20 to pass. This directly answers: “what’s the probability my selected config is overfit?”
Gate 6 — Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado): - Adjusts observed Sharpe for: non-normality (skew/kurtosis), series length, and number of variants tried (from the miner’s trial accounting, Module 3). - Pass: DSR > 0 with p < 0.05.
15.4 Kill list & archive
Every verdict recorded in runs.status with reason code (OOS_FAIL, WF_FAIL, MC_DD, PBO_HIGH, DSR_FAIL, REGIME_FRAGILE). Killed strategies are never deleted — the database of why things fail is itself research gold (you’ll notice patterns: “breakouts always die in low-vol regimes”).

17. Module 9 — Risk Engine
17.1 Purpose
Convert “we have an edge” into “we survive long enough to compound it.” The Risk Engine owns: position size, exposure limits, VaR estimation, and circuit breakers. Nothing sizes a trade except this module.
17.2 Position sizing (per trade)
risk_per_trade_pct   = base_risk × confidence_mult × decay_mult
base_risk            = 0.75% of current equity (range 0.5–1.0% by tier)
confidence_mult      = 0.75 (provisional) | 1.0 (validated) | 1.25 (validated + 100 live trades confirming)
decay_mult           = edge-decay detector output, 0.0–1.0 (§20)

qty = (equity × risk_per_trade_pct) / (stop_distance_pct)
      then × portfolio_weight (from Module 8)
Optional upgrade (after 6 months of data): volatility targeting — scale all sizes so forecast portfolio vol (GARCH via arch) ≈ 12% annualized; fractional-Kelly (¼) cross-check cap.
17.3 Exposure & circuit breakers (hard limits, code-enforced)
17.4 Risk metrics (dashboard + weekly report)
Historical VaR/CVaR at 95% & 99% (daily)
GARCH(1,1) forecast vol & parametric VaR (arch)
Rolling 30d/90d Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar

C2.3 Starter Strategy Playbook
Eight hand-built strategies to seed the system before the miner takes over. Each is specified completely enough to code without further design decisions.
S1 — Session-Open Momentum Breakout (Momentum)
Hypothesis: liquidity and directional flows concentrate after session opens; breaks of the opening range continue.
Universe/TF: BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT, SOLUSDT; 15m.
Setup: define first-hour range of europe session (07:00–08:00 UTC).
Entry (long): close above range high + 0.1×ATR buffer, within 3 hours of range close. Short symmetric.
Filters: ADX > 20; vol_regime ∈ {mid, high}; event blackout 30 min; no weekend.
Exit: SL = 1×ATR; TP = 2R; time stop 12 bars; hard exit at session end.
Expected profile: WR ~38–45%, PF ~1.3–1.5, 8–15 trades/pair/month.
S2 — VWAP Stretch Reversion (Mean Reversion)
Hypothesis: price over-extends from session VWAP and mean-reverts intraday.
Universe/TF: top-10 liquid pairs; 5m/15m.
Entry (long): close ≤ VWAP − 2σ(VWAP bands) AND wick rejection (range_pos < 0.25). Short symmetric.
Filters: trend_regime = range; vol_regime ∈ {low, mid}; event blackout; session ∈ {europe, us}.
Exit: TP = VWAP touch; SL = 1×ATR beyond extreme; time stop 16 bars.
Profile: WR ~55–65%, small avg win, needs tight cost control.
S3 — RSI-2 Pullback in Trend (Mean Reversion, Connors-style)
Hypothesis: sharp short-term dips within uptrends get bought.
Universe/TF: 10 pairs; 1h.
Entry (long): close > ema200 AND rsi_2 < 5 → buy next open. Short: close < ema200 AND rsi_2 > 95.
Filters: vol_regime ≠ extreme; F16 cooldown 24h.
Exit: rsi_2 > 70 (long) or time stop 48 bars; SL 2×ATR.
Profile: WR ~60%+, holds hours–2 days.
S4 — Donchian Trend-Following (Trend)
Hypothesis: long lookback breakouts capture multi-day trends; classic turtle logic works on majors.
Universe/TF: 20 pairs (breadth matters here); 4h/1d.
Entry: close beyond 55-bar Donchian extreme.
Filters: ADX rising; dollar_vol floor; none else (trend systems want few filters).
Exit: 20-bar opposite Donchian touch OR 3×ATR chandelier trail.
Profile: WR ~30–38%, fat right tail; the portfolio’s regime-diversifier.
S5 — Funding-Extreme Fade (Sentiment)
Hypothesis: extreme positive funding = overcrowded longs → fade; symmetric for negative.
Universe/TF: perp markets of 10 pairs; 1h/4h.
Entry (short): funding_z > 2.5 AND price fails to make new high on the funding print. Long symmetric.
Filters: vol_regime ∈ {mid, high}; trend_regime ≠ strong trend against fade.
Exit: funding normalizes (|z| < 0.5) or 2×ATR stop; time stop 72h.
Profile: event-driven, 5–10 trades/pair/month.
S6 — Post-Event Drift (Event)
Hypothesis: after scheduled macro events (FOMC/CPI), the initial move continues for hours.
Universe/TF: BTC, ETH; 5m/15m.
Entry: 30 min after event, direction = sign of post-event return if |move| > 1×ATR(1d)/√24.
Filters: only high-impact events; spread normal; no chop (first 30 min range not reversing).
Exit: TP 1.5R; SL 1R; hard time stop end of us session.
Profile: ~2–4 events/month; small n — needs years of data to validate.
S7 — Range Oscillator (Mean Reversion)
Hypothesis: in confirmed ranges, stochastics extremes revert.
Universe/TF: 10 pairs; 15m/1h.
Entry (long): trend_regime=range AND stoch_k < 15 AND stoch_k crosses above stoch_d. Short symmetric at 85.
Filters: bb_width pctile < 60 (actually ranging); session ≠ asia-thin hours optional (test).
Exit: mid-BB or stoch opposite extreme; SL 0.8×ATR; time stop 24 bars.
S8 — Month-End Flow Drift (Calendar)
Hypothesis: institutional rebalancing creates directional drift in the last 2 days of month.
Universe/TF: BTC, ETH; 1h/4h.
Entry: direction = sign of month’s return (continuation), entered on second-to-last day.
Filters: month’s |return| > 5%; no event blackout clash.
Exit: end of first day of new month; SL 1.5×ATR.
Profile: only ~1 trade/pair/month — validate on full history; small n, treat as provisional forever unless cross-market confirmed.
Playbook governance: these eight are hypotheses with specifications, not truths. Each enters the same Validation Lab gauntlet as miner output. Expect 3–5 of the 8 to survive — and expect the miner’s discoveries to replace most of them within a year.

C2.6 Testing Cookbook
C2.6.1 The no-lookahead test pattern (use for every feature/block)
def test_no_lookahead(feature_fn, sample_bars):
    full = feature_fn(sample_bars)
    for cut in [100, 500, len(sample_bars) - 10]:
        partial = feature_fn(sample_bars.iloc[:cut])
        pd.testing.assert_series_equal(
            full.iloc[:cut - 1], partial.iloc[:cut - 1],
            check_names=False)   # last partial bar may be incomplete
Any failure = the feature peeks into the future = would have poisoned every backtest using it.
C2.6.2 The synthetic-fixture pattern (use for every indicator)
Build tiny series by hand where the answer is known:
bars = make_bars(closes=[10, 11, 12, 13, 14])   # steady rise
assert rsi(bars, n=2).iloc[-1] == pytest.approx(100.0, abs=1e-6)
Five such fixtures per indicator beat 500 lines of “looks right” plotting.
C2.6.3 The known-strategy parity pattern (engines)
Buy-and-hold over 10 fixed windows with fees=0 must match qty × (exit − entry) to the cent. With fees=5bps it must be exactly 2 × notional × 0.0005 lower. Any engine that can’t reproduce arithmetic can’t be trusted with strategies.
C2.6.4 The negative-control pattern (miner, edge engine, validation lab)
rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
noise_trades = pd.DataFrame({"pnl_r": rng.normal(0, 1, 5000), ...labels...})
cards = scan_dimensions(noise_trades, dims=["hour_utc", "day_of_week"])
assert (cards.p_adj < 0.05).mean() < 0.02   # false-positive rate under control
Run after ANY change to statistics code. If random data produces “edges,” the lab is broken and every prior result is suspect.
C2.6.5 The positive-control pattern
Inject a known edge (e.g., +0.25R whenever day_of_week == 2) into noise. The edge engine must surface exactly day=2 in its top results; the validation lab must pass the synthetic strategy. If the system can’t find a planted edge, it won’t find real ones.
C2.6.6 The idempotency pattern (writers, updaters)
n1 = write_trades(run_id, trades)   # first write
n2 = write_trades(run_id, trades)   # same write again
assert n1 == len(trades) and n2 == 0

C2.7 Operational Playbooks & Incident Response
Pre-written responses so bad days have procedures, not panic.
PB-1: Daily-loss breaker fired 1. Breaker flattens and locks (automatic). 2. Do not override. 3. Journal what happened while fresh. 4. Classify: normal variance (most likely — two −1.5% days/month are expected at 0.75% risk) vs execution errors vs edge problem. 5. Only edge-problem classification feeds the weekly review; variance days are filed and forgotten.
PB-2: Strategy benched by decay detector 1. Note the z-score path in the strategy’s log. 2. Check regime context: favored regime absent (wait, size 0, keep monitoring) or regime present + still losing (edge death candidate). 3. Schedule re-validation in the monthly cycle. 4. Two failed re-validations → retire, archive reasons.
PB-3: Portfolio DD −12% (halving event) 1. Sizes halve automatically. 2. Operator re-reads §3 and §29. 3. Full report: which strategies contributed, live-vs-model drift, breaker history. 4. No new strategies, no re-mines deployed until DD < −6%. 5. Restore full size only after DD < −6% sustained 2 weeks.
PB-4: Portfolio DD −20% (full stop) 1. Everything flat, alerts disarmed. 2. Minimum 2-week cooling period — no trading, analysis only. 3. Full system audit: data integrity, cost realism, validation controls, journal adherence. 4. Written post-mortem before any restart. 5. Restart = back to paper for 4 weeks minimum, at half size.
PB-5: Exchange outage / API failure 1. Health monitor alerts. 2. Open positions: check via exchange app directly; if unmanageable, reduce exposure when possible. 3. Log outage window; affected trades flagged data_suspect in journal. 4. Resume only after data backfill passes validation.
PB-6: Data corruption detected 1. Validator quarantines affected pair-months. 2. Identify downstream runs using corrupt range → mark runs suspect in registry. 3. Re-download from archive, re-validate, re-run affected backtests. 4. Post-incident note in decision log.
PB-7: Discipline breach (traded without a card) 1. Trade journaled as violation (no hiding — the journal is for truth). 2. Third in a week → lockout engages. 3. Weekly review examines the trigger (boredom? revenge? FOMO?) and one countermeasure is adopted. 4. Pattern of breaches = size reduction until adherence ≥ 95% for a month.
PB-8: Winning streak euphoria (the quiet killer) 1. If trailing 20-trade win rate > backtest 75th percentile → expect mean reversion, do NOT increase size. 2. Sizing-up law (§30.1) still governs — streaks never override it. 3. Sweep profits to self-custody on schedule, not on feelings.

import io, zipfile, requests, pandas as pd
from pathlib import Path

BASE = "https://data.binance.vision/data/spot/monthly/klines"
COLS = ["open_time","open","high","low","close","volume",
        "close_time","quote_vol","trades","taker_buy","taker_buy_q","ignore"]

def months_between(start: str, end: str) -> list[str]:
    return [str(p.date())[:7] for p in
            pd.period_range(start, end, freq="M")]

def fetch_month(pair: str, tf: str, ym: str) -> pd.DataFrame | None:
    url = f"{BASE}/{pair}/{tf}/{pair}-{tf}-{ym}.zip"
    r = requests.get(url, timeout=60)
    if r.status_code == 404:
        return None                      # pair not listed yet
    r.raise_for_status()
    with zipfile.ZipFile(io.BytesIO(r.content)) as z:
        df = pd.read_csv(z.open(z.namelist()[0]), header=None, names=COLS)
    df["open_time"] = pd.to_datetime(df["open_time"], unit="ms", utc=True)
    return df.drop(columns=["close_time", "taker_buy_q", "ignore"])

# src/datalake/resample.py
AGG = {"open":"first","high":"max","low":"min","close":"last",
       "volume":"sum","quote_vol":"sum","trades":"sum","taker_buy":"sum"}

def resample(df_1m: pd.DataFrame, tf: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    rule = {"5m":"5min","15m":"15min","1h":"1h","4h":"4h","1d":"1D"}[tf]
    out = (df_1m.set_index("open_time")
                .resample(rule, origin="epoch")
                .agg(AGG)
                .dropna(subset=["open"]))   # gaps produce NaN rows → drop
    return out.reset_index()

import duckdb, pandas as pd

def write_trades(con: duckdb.DuckDBPyConnection, run_id: str,
                 trades: pd.DataFrame, features: pd.DataFrame) -> int:
    df = trades.copy()
    df["run_id"] = run_id
    # --- derive edge labels by joining features at entry_time ---
    df = df.merge(features, left_on=["pair","entry_time"],
                  right_on=["pair","open_time"], how="left")
    df["hour_utc"]    = df["entry_time"].dt.hour
    df["day_of_week"] = df["entry_time"].dt.dayofweek
    df["session"]     = df["hour_utc"].map(session_of_hour)
    # trend_regime, vol_regime, rsi_at_entry, ... come from the merge
    before = con.execute("SELECT COUNT(*) FROM trades").fetchone()[0]
    con.register("df_stage", df)
    con.execute("""
        INSERT INTO trades BY NAME SELECT * FROM df_stage
        ON CONFLICT (trade_id) DO NOTHING
    """)
    after = con.execute("SELECT COUNT(*) FROM trades").fetchone()[0]
    return after - before        # idempotency test asserts re-write == 0
C2.11.4 Slice statistics engine
# src/edge/slice.py
import pandas as pd
from scipy import stats

def slice_stats(df: pd.DataFrame, min_n: int = 30) -> dict:
    n = len(df)
    if n < min_n:
        return {"n": n, "reliable": False}
    r = df["pnl_r"]
    wins, losses = r[r > 0], r[r <= 0]
    t_stat, p_val = stats.ttest_1samp(r, 0)
    return {
        "n": n, "reliable": n >= 100,
        "expectancy_r": r.mean(), "std_r": r.std(),
        "win_rate": (r > 0).mean(),
        "profit_factor": wins.sum() / max(-losses.sum(), 1e-12),
        "avg_win_r": wins.mean() if len(wins) else 0,
        "avg_loss_r": losses.mean() if len(losses) else 0,
        "max_consec_loss": max_consecutive(r <= 0),
        "t_stat": t_stat, "p_value": p_val,
        "ci95": bootstrap_ci(r.values, n_boot=5000),
    }

def max_consecutive(loss_mask: pd.Series) -> int:
    groups = (~loss_mask).cumsum()
    return loss_mask.groupby(groups).sum().max() if len(loss_mask) else 0
C2.11.5 Multiple-testing correction (Benjamini–Hochberg)
# src/edge/significance.py
import numpy as np, pandas as pd

def bh_adjust(scan_df: pd.DataFrame, q: float = 0.05) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = scan_df.sort_values("p_value").reset_index(drop=True)
    m = len(df)
    df["bh_threshold"] = (df.index + 1) / m * q
    df["significant"] = df["p_value"] <= df["bh_threshold"]
    # enforce monotonicity: significance cut at last passing index
    if df["significant"].any():
        cut = df.index[df["significant"]].max()
        df["significant"] = df.index <= cut
    return df.sort_index()
C2.11.6 Monte Carlo battery
# src/validation/montecarlo.py
import numpy as np

def mc_reshuffle(pnl_r: np.ndarray, sims: int = 10_000,
                 rng: np.random.Generator = None) -> dict:
    rng = rng or np.random.default_rng()
    max_dds = np.empty(sims); finals = np.empty(sims)
    for i in range(sims):
        curve = np.cumsum(rng.permutation(pnl_r))
        peak = np.maximum.accumulate(curve)
        max_dds[i] = (peak - curve).max()
        finals[i] = curve[-1]
    return {"dd_p50": np.percentile(max_dds, 50),
            "dd_p95": np.percentile(max_dds, 95),
            "dd_p99": np.percentile(max_dds, 99),
            "final_p05": np.percentile(finals, 5),
            "sims": sims}

def mc_skip(pnl_r: np.ndarray, skip_rate: float, sims=10_000, rng=None):
    rng = rng or np.random.default_rng()
    keep = lambda: pnl_r[rng.random(len(pnl_r)) > skip_rate]
    exp = np.array([keep().mean() for _ in range(sims) if len(keep())])
    return {"median_expectancy": np.median(exp),
            "p05_expectancy": np.percentile(exp, 5)}

def mc_block_bootstrap(pnl_r: np.ndarray, block: int = 20,
                       sims: int = 10_000, rng=None):
    rng = rng or np.random.default_rng()
    n_blocks = len(pnl_r) // block
    blocks = pnl_r[: n_blocks * block].reshape(n_blocks, block)
    dds = []
    for _ in range(sims):
        sample = blocks[rng.integers(0, n_blocks, n_blocks)].ravel()
        curve = np.cumsum(sample)
        dds.append((np.maximum.accumulate(curve) - curve).max())
    return {"dd_p95": np.percentile(dds, 95)}
C2.11.7 Walk-forward engine
# src/validation/walkforward.py
import pandas as pd

def anchored_folds(start: str, end: str, train_years=3,
                   test_months=6) -> list[tuple]:
    folds, train_start = [], pd.Timestamp(start)
    end_ts = pd.Timestamp(end)
    while True:
        train_end = train_start + pd.DateOffset(years=train_years)
        test_end  = train_end + pd.DateOffset(months=test_months)
        if test_end > end_ts:
            break
        folds.append((train_start, train_end, train_end, test_end))
        train_start += pd.DateOffset(months=test_months)  # rolling anchor
    return folds

def walk_forward(strategy_cfg, folds, optimize_fn, evaluate_fn) -> dict:
    oos_curves, metrics = [], []
    for (tr_s, tr_e, te_s, te_e) in folds:
        best = optimize_fn(strategy_cfg, tr_s, tr_e)     # IS: pick plateau best
        m = evaluate_fn(best, te_s, te_e)                # OOS: untouched data
        metrics.append(m); oos_curves.append(m["equity"])
    is_ann  = np.mean([m["is_ann_return"] for m in metrics])
    oos_ann = np.mean([m["oos_ann_return"] for m in metrics])
    return {"wfe": oos_ann / max(is_ann, 1e-9),
            "folds": metrics,
            "stitched_oos": pd.concat(oos_curves)}
C2.11.8 Edge-decay detector
# src/monitoring/decay.py
import pandas as pd, numpy as np

def decay_z(live_trades: pd.DataFrame, bt_mean: float,
            bt_std: float, window: int = 30) -> dict:
    r = live_trades.sort_values("entry_time")["pnl_r"].tail(window)
    if len(r) < 10:
        return {"status": "insufficient", "n": len(r)}
    z = (r.mean() - bt_mean) / (bt_std / np.sqrt(len(r)) + 1e-12)
    status = ("healthy" if z > -1.5 else
              "watch"   if z > -2.0 else
              "halve"   if z > -2.5 else "bench")
    return {"status": status, "z": round(z, 2), "n": len(r),
            "roll_expectancy": round(r.mean(), 3)}

import pandas as pd

def hrp_weights(strategy_returns: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.Series:
    model = HierarchicalRiskParity()
    model.fit(strategy_returns)
    w = pd.Series(model.weights_, index=strategy_returns.columns)
    return apply_constraints(w)     # §16.3 caps + correlation guard

def apply_constraints(w: pd.Series, cap=0.30) -> pd.Series:
    w = w.clip(upper=cap)
    return w / w.sum()              # renormalize after capping

22. Formula & Metrics Library
Expectancy (R): E[R] = (WR × avg_win_R) − ((1−WR) × avg_loss_R) — must be > 0 net of costs.
Profit factor: PF = gross_profit / |gross_loss|; ≥ 1.3 to survive screening, ≥ 1.15 OOS.
Sharpe (annualized): SR = mean(r_daily) / std(r_daily) × √365 (crypto trades 365 days).
Sortino: downside deviation only; preferred for skewed crypto returns.
Calmar: CAGR / |maxDD|; ≥ 1.0 target at portfolio level.
Max drawdown: max_t (peak_t − equity_t) / peak_t on the equity curve.
Walk-Forward Efficiency: WFE = ann_return_OOS / ann_return_IS; ≥ 0.5.
Deflated Sharpe Ratio: DSR = Φ( (SR̂ − E[max SR under H0]) / SE(SR̂) ) where E[max SR] grows with √(2 ln N) for N variants tried, adjusted for skew/kurtosis — full formula implemented from Bailey & López de Prado (2014).
PBO: fraction of CSCV combinations where IS-optimal variant ranks below OOS median; logit transformation per López de Prado (2018).
Historical VaR_α: quantile(r_daily, 1−α); CVaR_α: mean of returns beyond VaR.
Parametric VaR (GARCH): VaR_95 = μ̂ + σ̂_t+1 × z_0.05, σ̂ from GARCH(1,1) via arch.
Position size: qty = equity × risk% / stop_distance% (× portfolio weight × decay multiplier).
Kelly fraction: f* = WR − (1−WR)/payoff; deploy at most f*/4.
Kupiec VaR backtest: LR = −2 ln[(1−p)^(T−N) p^N] + 2 ln[(1−N/T)^(T−N) (N/T)^N] ~ χ²(1).
Multiple-testing corrections: Bonferroni α/m; Benjamini–Hochberg FDR at q=0.05 (default for scans).

Appendix A — Glossary
Alpha — return attributable to skill/edge rather than market exposure. Backtest — simulation of a strategy on historical data. Circuit breaker — pre-committed automatic risk limit. CVaR (Expected Shortfall) — average loss beyond the VaR threshold. Deflated Sharpe Ratio — Sharpe significance adjusted for multiple testing and non-normality. Drawdown — peak-to-trough equity decline. Edge Card — operational specification of when/where a validated strategy may trade. Expectancy — average profit per trade in R or bps, net of costs. HRP — Hierarchical Risk Parity allocation. IC (Information Coefficient) — correlation between signal and forward returns. In-sample / Out-of-sample — data used for discovery vs data reserved for testing. MAE/MFE — max adverse/favorable excursion during a trade. PBO — Probability of Backtest Overfitting (CSCV method). Profit factor — gross profit ÷ gross loss. R-multiple — trade P&L expressed in units of initial risk. Regime — persistent market state (trend/volatility). Slippage — difference between expected and actual fill price. Walk-forward analysis — rolling optimize→test validation producing a stitched OOS curve. WFE — Walk-Forward Efficiency (OOS/IS return ratio).

Appendix G — Worked Mathematics & Sizing Workbook
Numeric examples you’ll reuse constantly. Verify your implementation against these by hand.
G.1 Expectancy arithmetic
Strategy: WR 44%, avg win +1.9R, avg loss −1.0R, round-trip cost 0.06R.
E[R] = 0.44 × 1.9 − 0.56 × 1.0 − 0.06 = 0.836 − 0.56 − 0.06 = +0.216R/trade
At 0.75% risk/trade and 60 trades/month: E[monthly] ≈ 60 × 0.216 × 0.75% ≈ +9.7%/month
At 0.75% risk/trade and 8 trades/month:  E[monthly] ≈ +1.3%/month
Lesson: expectancy × frequency × size = income. Filters trade frequency for expectancy — the edge analysis exists to optimize that product, not any single term.
G.2 Position sizing (three examples)
Equity $4,000; risk 0.75% → $30 at risk. - Ex 1: ETHUSDT, entry 3,200, stop 3,168 (1% below). qty = 30 / 0.01 = $3,000 notional ≈ 0.937 ETH. Portfolio weight 0.5 (if applied) → 0.469 ETH. - Ex 2: BTCUSDT, stop distance 0.4%. qty = 30 / 0.004 = $7,500 notional. Same risk, bigger size — stop distance sets size, never the reverse. - Ex 3: decay_mult 0.5 active → $15 at risk → halve whichever notional the formula gives.
G.3 Kelly criterion sanity check
WR 45%, payoff 1.8. f* = 0.45 − 0.55/1.8 = 0.144 → quarter-Kelly = 3.6% of equity per trade — far above our 0.75% fixed-fractional. Conclusion: fixed-fractional is the conservative governor; Kelly is only ever a ceiling cross-check, never the sizing engine at our sample sizes.
G.4 Monte Carlo interpretation
10,000 reshuffles of a 600-trade sequence with maxDD realized 11%:
MC maxDD percentiles:  50th 12% | 75th 15% | 90th 18% | 95th 21% | 99th 27%
Reading: realized 11% sits at the ~40th percentile — strategy behaved as expected. If live DD reaches 19%, that’s the ~93rd percentile: not proof of death, but decay detector should already be halving size. The MC band converts “drawdown panic” into a percentile reading.
G.5 VaR & CVaR by hand
Daily portfolio returns over 500 days, sorted worst 25 (= 5%): −3.2% … −1.9%. - Historical VaR95 ≈ 1.9% (the 25th-worst day boundary) - CVaR95 ≈ mean of worst 25 ≈ −2.5% - GARCH parametric: σ̂(t+1) = 1.15% → VaR95 = 1.645 × 1.15% ≈ 1.89% (consistent ✓) - Kupiec check: expected 25 exceptions, observed 28 → within acceptance region (don’t reject model).
G.6 Deflated Sharpe intuition
Same strategy, same Sharpe 1.4, two contexts: - Tried 10 variants → E[max SR | H0] ≈ 0.8 → DSR comfortably positive → PASS - Tried 100,000 variants → E[max SR | H0] ≈ 1.5 → DSR ≈ 0 → FAIL Identical performance, opposite verdicts — because luck’s expected maximum grows with trials. This is why the miner’s trial accounting (n_variants) is sacred.
G.7 Risk-of-ruin framing
With E[R] = +0.2, σ(R) = 1.2, risk 0.75%/trade: risk of hitting the −20% stop before doubling is small (single digits %). At 3% risk/trade it multiplies several-fold. The 0.5–1% band isn’t timidity; it’s what keeps the −20% event rare enough that PB-4 is a procedure you’ll likely never run.

33. Statistics Primer for Traders
The minimum statistics you must understand to operate this system honestly. Each concept is stated, then translated into trading terms.
33.1 Distributions. Trade P&L is a random draw from the strategy’s return distribution. Crypto trade returns are fat-tailed (extreme moves more common than a normal distribution predicts) and often skewed (trend systems: many small losses, few large wins — negative skew of losses, positive skew of P&L tail). Consequence: never use normal-distribution assumptions for risk; use historical/bootstrapped distributions (which is exactly why our Monte Carlo reshuffles real trades instead of simulating Gaussians).
33.2 Sample size and standard error. The standard error of a mean shrinks as 1/√n. A slice of 25 trades with expectancy +0.3R and σ(R)=1.2 has SE ≈ 0.24 — the “edge” is 1.25 standard errors from zero, i.e., indistinguishable from luck. At n=100 the same numbers give SE ≈ 0.12 (2.5σ — worth attention); at n=400, 5σ (real). This single formula — z = E / (σ/√n) — is the mathematical spine of the n≥100 rule, the decay detector, and the live-vs-backtest drift test.
33.3 Hypothesis testing. Null hypothesis H0: “this slice has no edge (true mean = 0).” The p-value is P(data this extreme | H0 true) — NOT the probability the edge is real. p < 0.05 means: if there were no edge, data like this would appear only 5% of the time. With one test, that’s persuasive. With 500 tests (one hour×day scan), you EXPECT ~25 false positives at p<0.05 — which is why §33.5 exists.
33.4 Confidence intervals. “Expectancy +0.21R, 95% CI [+0.07, +0.35]” beats “expectancy +0.21R” every time. The CI tells you the plausible range; if it crosses zero, the estimate is noise-grade regardless of how good the point looks. Bootstrap CIs (resample the trades 5,000×, take percentiles) handle the fat tails correctly and are built into the slice engine.
33.5 Multiple comparisons and FDR. Every additional hypothesis tested inflates the false-positive rate. Corrections: Bonferroni (divide α by number of tests — simple, conservative) and Benjamini–Hochberg (controls the expected fraction of false discoveries — more powerful, our default). The edge engine never reports a raw p-value without its correction context. Rule of thumb: if you scanned 168 hour×day cells, treat any unadjusted p < 0.05 as meaningless; only BH-passing slices may become cards.
33.6 Regression to the mean. Extreme observed performance (the best variant of 100,000) is almost always part skill, part luck — and the luck part evaporates forward. Expect OOS performance to be worse than IS, systematically (that’s why WFE ≥ 0.5 passes but WFE = 1.0 should make you suspicious of a leak).
33.7 Stationarity. Statistics assumes the distribution stays stable. Markets are non-stationary — vol clusters, regimes shift, edges die. This is THE fundamental problem of quantitative trading, and the system’s entire monitoring layer (decay detection, monthly re-validation, card expiry) is the engineered response to it.
33.8 Independence and autocorrelation. Trades cluster in time; a losing streak is not 10 independent losses but often one bad regime. Block bootstrap (MC #3) exists because reshuffle assumes independence; when the two disagree, trust the block version.
33.9 Bayesian intuition (no math required). Prior: most strategy ideas fail (>90%). Evidence: a good backtest. Posterior: still probably fails — the validation gauntlet is how you accumulate enough evidence to update a skeptical prior. Operators who internalize this stop falling in love with backtests.
33.10 The two errors. Type I: trading a strategy that doesn’t work (costs money). Type II: discarding one that does (costs opportunity). Retail optimizes against Type II (afraid to miss anything); professionals optimize against Type I (afraid to trade noise). This system is deliberately Type-I-averse: capital preservation beats missed chances, always.
34. Crypto Market Microstructure Primer
What you must know about the terrain you’re trading.
34.1 Perpetuals vs spot. Perps track spot via the funding mechanism: when perp trades above spot, longs pay shorts (positive funding) and vice versa. Funding is a direct sentiment gauge (extreme = crowded positioning → S5 fade strategy) and a real cash flow if you hold perp positions. Spot has no funding but also no short option on most retail venues — most short strategies in this system assume perp execution.
34.2 Liquidity cycles. Despite 24/7 trading, volume follows a daily wave: deepest during the Europe–US overlap (roughly 12:00–16:00 UTC), thinnest in late-Asia hours and weekends. Consequences: (a) slippage varies 2–3× across the day — the cost model’s session-varying slippage is not optional; (b) breakout strategies work best when participation arrives (session opens); (c) weekend price action is lower-quality — always test weekend exclusion as a filter.
34.3 Volatility regimes. Crypto vol clusters hard: quiet weeks of 1% daily ranges punctuated by 10% days. ATR-percentile regime labels capture this. Mean-reversion strategies harvest the quiet; momentum strategies harvest the storms; a strategy blind to vol regime is a coin flip on which world it’s in.
34.4 Event structure. Macro events (FOMC, US CPI, payrolls) hit crypto sharply — the 30 minutes after release are spread-widening, wick-heavy chaos. Trade them only via a dedicated event strategy (S6) or stand aside (F08 blackout). Token-specific events (unlocks, mainnet launches, exchange listings) matter for alts.
34.5 Cross-pair correlation. Alts are highly correlated to BTC (typical 30d correlation 0.6–0.9). Five alt longs ≈ one big BTC beta position — the correlation guard and max same-direction limits exist for exactly this. It also means multi-pair “diversification” is partly illusory; true diversification comes from strategy family diversity (trend vs MR vs carry).
34.6 Exchange microstructure. Binance spot taker fees ~10bps (less with BNB discount/volume tiers — recalibrate config to YOUR actual tier), perp taker ~4–5bps. Market orders cross the spread; limit orders earn it but risk non-fill. In backtests, always pay taker costs unless the strategy is explicitly limit-based with a fill model — optimism here is the classic way backtests lie.
34.7 Weekend and holiday effects. Institutional participation drops on weekends and US holidays; ranges compress but stop-hunts in thin books produce vicious wicks. Test, don’t assume: some pairs show real weekend edges; most show noise with worse fills.
34.8 Stablecoin basis and risk. P&L in USDT is P&L in a dollar-IOU. Operationally: keep balances modest, sweep profits, and remember depeg tail-risk exists. The system’s custody rule (monthly sweeps) is non-negotiable.

38. Self-Interrogation FAQ
The questions you’ll secretly ask yourself at 2 AM, answered in advance.
“The backtest is incredible — why shouldn’t I just trade it now?” Because an unvalidated backtest is a hypothesis, not a result. Run the gauntlet. If it’s real, it will still be real in two weeks.
“The miner found nothing this month — is it broken?” Check the controls (A7.3/A7.4). If controls pass, nothing-found is a legitimate market state. Mining dry spells are normal; forcing output is how noise gets promoted.
“A strategy passed everything but feels wrong.” Write down why. If the reason is mechanical (“I can’t explain the logic”), it doesn’t trade — the 60-second rule. If it’s vibes, trade it small; feelings aren’t evidence either.
“Three losing weeks — is the edge dead?” Compute the z-score (the dashboard does). A −1.5% week happens at 0.75% risk with no edge problems at all. The detector tells you when it’s statistically meaningful; until then, variance is the boring answer.
“Can I just add one discretionary trade?” That’s violation #1 toward lockout. The system exists because discretionary-you underperforms systematic-you. Journal the urge instead — urges logged weekly are useful psychology data.
“Why not just buy a commercial tool (StrategyQuant etc.)?” Budget is zero, and black-box mining without the validation literacy this build gives you would lose money anyway. You’re building the tool AND the operator.
“When do I scale up?” §30.1’s sizing-up law. Never earlier, never because of a hot streak, never because of impatience.
“What if everything fails after a year?” Then you’ll have something worth more than a strategy: a verified negative result set, institutional-grade process skills, and a database of 100k+ analyzed trades — assets that transfer to any future trading, quant job applications, or fund-raising credibility. The only true failure mode is skipping the process.
39. Universe & Correlation Management
The 20-pair universe is a managed portfolio in itself, not a fixed list.
39.1 Admission rules. A pair enters the research universe when: (a) ≥ 2 years of history on Binance; (b) 20-day average dollar volume > $20M (liquidity floor); (c) not a stablecoin/fiat pair; (d) not within 30 days of a known delisting. New listings wait 6 months before admission (listing-phase price action is its own chaotic regime).
39.2 Removal rules. Quarterly review removes pairs that: fall below the liquidity floor for 60+ days, get delisted, or develop data-quality issues that fail remediation. Removed pairs stay in the historical archive (their data remains valid for research) but leave the live universe.
39.3 Survivorship discipline. When researching cross-pair effects, include pairs that were delisted during the sample (pull them from the archive before removal). A universe of only today’s winners inflates every cross-sectional result.
39.4 Correlation management. Maintain a rolling 90-day BTC-correlation table for all pairs (auto-computed weekly). Uses: (a) the same-direction exposure limiter counts correlated longs as one position; (b) HRP clustering treats high-correlation pairs as one risk family; (c) when adding a new pair, prefer one whose strategy returns correlate < 0.5 with existing ones — diversification is measured on strategy returns, not tickers.
39.5 Universe tiers. T1 core (BTC, ETH — deepest liquidity, first to trade live), T2 majors (next 8 by volume), T3 satellites (rest — research only until Tier T2 capital). Live trading expands tier-by-tier only after paper results confirm per-tier slippage assumptions.
39.6 Pair-strategy fit matrix. Maintain a table of strategy × pair expectancy from the trade DB; review quarterly. Pairs where a strategy persistently fails get excluded from that strategy (documented as a filter), and the matrix often reveals structure (e.g., mean-reversion works on low-beta alts, momentum on high-beta) that feeds new miner filter ideas.

41. Case Studies — Anatomy of a Failure & of a Success
Two composite case studies showing the system thinking end-to-end. Expect your own versions of both within the first year.
41.1 Anatomy of a failure: “The Perfect Tuesday”
Discovery. A Stage-2 mining run surfaces T05+F05(Tue)+X02(2R) on BNBUSDT: expectancy +0.61R, n=88, PF 2.1. The Tuesday-only filter looks like magic.
Red flags the system raises automatically: 1. n=88 < 100 → provisional, can’t be carded. Operator temptation: “close enough.” It is not. 2. The scanner tested 168 day×hour cells × 500+ combos — BH correction requires p < 0.0012; this slice sits at p = 0.008. Fails FDR. On noise alone we’d expect several cells this good. 3. Stability check: 100% of the edge comes from the 2021 bull half; the 2022 half is flat. Single-regime dependence. 4. Plateau: moving the day filter to Mon or Wed → negative. The “edge” is one cell wide — a spike, not a plateau.
Verdict: KILLED at Gate 1-adjacent checks; archived with reason MULTIPLE_TESTING + REGIME_FRAGILE. Cost of this discipline: nothing. Cost without it: ~3 months and a real-money drawdown discovering the same thing.
Lesson the database keeps: six months later, monthly re-mine finds the same Tuesday pattern again — now it’s in the kill archive, cross-referenced, and auto-flagged. The system remembers so you don’t re-pay tuition.
41.2 Anatomy of a success: “Boring Session Breakout”
Discovery. T09 session-breakout variant on ETHUSDT (the §C2.4 walkthrough): +0.19R, n=631 — unimpressive next to the Tuesday miracle.
Why it survives: n large; plateau 0.875 (neighbors work); profitable in both halves; session-slice p_adj = 0.003 after BH; OOS +0.21R (expectancy went UP out-of-sample — happens; treat with same skepticism, verified no leak); WFE 0.71; MC dd_p95 13%; PBO 0.11; DSR p=0.02.
Life after validation: paper 41 trades +0.16R → live micro 60 trades +0.18R → decay z never below −1.2 → promoted to CORE at 100 live trades, sizing multiplier 1.25. Contributes a boring +1.2%/month at 0.75% risk.
The meta-lesson: the failure LOOKED better in every screenshot. The success looked boring in all of them. The system’s gates exist because humans cannot tell these apart by looking — the failed one had the higher backtest expectancy by 3×. This is the entire point of the architecture.
42. The First 100 Live Trades — What to Expect
A statistical preview so nothing surprises you.
Trade 1–20: Everything feels significant. It isn’t. n=20 has SE ≈ 0.27R — you could be +0.5R or −0.3R and both are consistent with your +0.2R backtest edge. Do not touch anything. Journal everything.
Trade 20–40: First real losing streak (60% chance of a 5+ streak by now — Appendix K.3). The decay detector may flicker to WATCH. Trade 40–70: The first breaker event likely fires (daily −1.5% happens monthly on average). Follow PB-1. Notice how different real-money losses feel vs paper — that feeling is exactly why the operator laws exist. Emotion scores in your journal will drift up; the weekly review will catch it.
Trade 70–100: Drift z-score becomes meaningful. Most likely outcome: live expectancy 60–90% of backtest (costs, fills, your latency). That’s success. If ≥ 60%: sizing law permits consideration of CORE promotion. If < 40%: the strategy goes back through validation — evidence, not disappointment.
After trade 100: You now own something almost no retail trader has — a statistically meaningful live track record, fully journaled, with known slippage, known discipline metrics, and a validated (or correctly rejected) edge. Everything after this is scaling and repetition.

Appendix N — Expanded Glossary of Quant Terms
Alpha decay — the tendency of a discovered edge to weaken as markets adapt. Anchored walk-forward — walk-forward where the training window’s start stays fixed while it grows. Autocorrelation — correlation of a series with its own past; streaks in trade P&L. Basis — price difference between perp and spot. Beta — sensitivity to the market factor (BTC for alts). Block bootstrap — resampling contiguous chunks to preserve autocorrelation. Breadth — number of independent bets; Grinold’s law: skill × √breadth. Calmar ratio — CAGR ÷ max drawdown. Chandelier exit — ATR-based trailing stop hanging from the highest high. Cointegration — long-run statistical linkage between non-stationary series (pairs trading basis). Cross-sectional — comparing many assets at one time (vs time-series: one asset across time). CSCV — combinatorially symmetric cross-validation; the PBO machinery. CVaR / Expected Shortfall — average loss in the worst α% of cases. Deflated Sharpe — Sharpe significance deflated by trial count and non-normality. Drawdown duration — time spent below the equity peak; hurts more than depth. Edge — positive expectancy that persists out-of-sample and net of costs. Embargo — gap enforced between train and test windows to prevent leakage. Expectancy — mean P&L per trade (R or bps). Exposure — fraction of time/capital in the market. Fat tails — extreme events more frequent than Gaussian prediction. Fill model — assumptions about how orders execute in backtest. Funding rate — perp mechanism payment anchoring perp to spot. GARCH — volatility clustering model; forecasts tomorrow’s vol. HRP — clustering-based allocation needing no covariance inversion. Information Coefficient — rank correlation of signal vs forward return. Intrabar ambiguity — unknowable order of high/low within one bar. Kelly criterion — growth-optimal bet fraction; used fractionally if at all. Kupiec test — statistical check of VaR model exception rate. Leakage — any future information contaminating the past; the original sin. MAE/MFE — worst/best excursion during a trade’s life. Multiple comparisons problem — more tests ⇒ more false discoveries. Overfitting — fitting noise; performs in-sample, dies live. PBO — probability the selected config is overfit. Plateau — parameter region where neighbors also perform. Point-in-time — data as known at that moment, no revisions. Profit factor — gross profit ÷ gross loss. Purged CV — cross-validation removing overlapping label windows. R-multiple — P&L in units of initial risk. Regime — persistent market state with stable statistics. Risk parity — allocating so each component contributes equal risk. Sharpe ratio — excess return per unit volatility. Slippage — execution price worse than decision price. Sortino — Sharpe using downside deviation only. Stationarity — stable statistical properties over time. Stochastic dominance — one distribution systematically better than another. Survivorship bias — measuring only what survived. Tail risk — exposure to extreme, rare losses. Time-stop — exit after fixed holding period. Turnover — trading frequency; drives cost drag. Type I/II error — trading noise / discarding signal. VaR — loss level not exceeded with probability α. Volatility targeting — scaling size to hold portfolio vol constant. Walk-forward efficiency — OOS ÷ IS annualized performance. Wick — bar extreme beyond body; rejection evidence. z-score — distance from mean in standard errors.

Final word. This plan is deliberately conservative: it assumes most ideas fail, that noise impersonates edge, and that the operator is human. Systems built on those assumptions are the ones still trading in year three. Build the heartbeat first (G2), protect the database like treasure, let the Validation Lab break your heart weekly — and let compounding do what compounding does.
