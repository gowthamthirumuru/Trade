L18: 1. Executive Summary
L19: Project APEX is a complete, zero-budget quantitative trading system that you will build and operate alone. Its purpose is not to “find a winning strategy” — it is to build a factory that continuously discovers, validates, filters, sizes, monitors, and retires strategies, the same loop every professional quant firm runs, rebuilt from free open-source components.
L20: The system rests on five pillars:
L21: A data lake of free, institutional-quality crypto market data (Binance public archive: tick-to-daily history since 2017, unlimited downloads, no account required), stored locally in Parquet + DuckDB.
L22: A strategy miner that automatically discovers candidate strategies — first by brute-force combinatorial search over indicator/filter/exit building blocks, later by genetic programming that evolves strategy logic itself, and finally by local machine learning (no paid AI APIs anywhere).
L23: A trade database (“the edge mine”) in which every trade from every backtest and every live session is stored with ~30 labels (strategy, parameters, pair, session, hour, day, volatility regime, trend regime, exit reason, MFE/MAE…). This database is the system’s most valuable permanent asset: it lets you mine when and where each strategy works — your original edge idea, implemented with statistical rigor.
L24: A validation lab that kills weak strategies before they cost money: walk-forward analysis, four kinds of Monte Carlo simulation, Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO), Deflated Sharpe Ratio, and regime stress tests. Expect to reject 95% of what the miner finds. That rejection rate is the system working correctly.
L25: A risk-first portfolio engine and command center: volatility-targeted position sizing, HRP/CVaR allocation across strategies, hard circuit breakers, and a Streamlit dashboard that hands you a clear “edge card” each session so manual execution becomes checklist execution.
L26: What success looks like (12–18 months): a system holding 100,000+ labeled trades; 5–10 validated strategies with out-of-sample-confirmed session/day/regime filters; a portfolio-level risk engine with circuit breakers you actually obey; a live track record of 100+ real trades whose expectancy matches the backtest within tolerance; and a monthly re-mining loop that keeps the edge fresh as markets evolve.
L27: Build time to first validated paper trade: ~12 weeks. To first live trade: ~5–6 months. Budget: ₹0 / $0.

L37: 3. Honest Constraints & Realistic Expectations
L38: This chapter exists so that month-6-you doesn’t quit out of misplaced expectations. Read it monthly.
L39: 3.1 You cannot beat institutions at their game. Funds have colocated servers, tick-level order flow, teams of PhDs, and billion-dollar data budgets. You will not out-speed or out-data them.
L40: 3.2 You can win the game they refuse to play. Institutional capital cannot bother with edges worth 0.3–1% per week on $5,000 — it’s meaningless to them and often too small or capacity-limited to trade. Retail-sized, patient, well-filtered edges in liquid-but-slow timeframes are exactly the niche this system targets.
L41: 3.3 Filtering turns break-even into profitable — it does not resurrect garbage. Day/hour/session/regime filtering (your core idea) reliably improves mediocre strategies; it rarely saves a strategy with no underlying edge. The miner’s job is to find raw material; the filter’s job is to refine it.
L42: 3.4 Most discovered “edges” are noise. If you test 100,000 variants, thousands look brilliant by pure chance. This is the multiple-comparisons problem, and it is why Part C’s Validation Lab (PBO, Deflated Sharpe, walk-forward) is non-negotiable. The uncomfortable truth: the better the backtest looks, the more skeptical you must be.
L43: 3.5 Edges decay. Market regimes shift; other participants arbitrage patterns away. Assume every edge has a half-life of 6–24 months. The monthly re-mining and edge-decay detector exist for this reason.
L44: 3.6 The operator is the biggest risk. Manual execution, revenge trading after losses, overriding the system “just this once,” stopping during drawdowns — operator behavior kills more retail systems than bad math. Chapter 29 is as important as any code.
L45: 3.7 Expectancy arithmetic (memorize):
L46: Expectancy = (win_rate × avg_win) − (loss_rate × avg_loss) − costs
L47: A strategy is tradeable only if Expectancy > 0 AFTER realistic
L48: fees + slippage + spread, AND n ≥ 100 OOS trades, AND it survives
L49: the Validation Lab.
L50: 3.8 Timeline honesty. Month 1–3: building, zero trading income. Month 4–6: paper trading, zero income. Month 6–12: small live trading, modest income if everything works. Compounding is slow at first and then not. Anyone promising faster is selling something.

L59: 5. The Twelve Core Design Principles
L60: These principles govern every build decision. When in doubt, the principle that says “be more conservative” wins.
L61: The database is the product. Strategies are disposable; the labeled trade database compounds in value forever. Never run a backtest whose trades aren’t logged.
L62: Costs first, always. Every backtest models fees, spread, and slippage from day one. A backtest without costs is fiction.
L63: In-sample is for searching, out-of-sample is for deciding. The miner never sees the test period. No exceptions, ever.
L64: Sample size or it didn’t happen. No slice, filter, or strategy is trusted below n=100 trades (50 = provisional only).
L65: Simple beats clever. A robust simple strategy beats a fragile complex one. Complexity must earn its place with OOS performance.
L66: Plateaus, not peaks. Choose parameter regions where neighbors also work; a lone peak is an overfit artifact.
L67: Kill ruthlessly. Rejecting 90–95% of candidates is the sign of a healthy pipeline, not a failure.
L68: Everything is reproducible. Every run is config-driven, seeded, logged, and re-runnable byte-for-byte.
L69: Local-first, zero-cost. If a component needs a paid API, it doesn’t belong in the core system.
L70: The system advises; discipline executes. Until auto-execution is earned, the human executes the checklist — not impulses.
L71: Risk is a budget, not an afterthought. Position size, exposure, and circuit breakers are computed by the Risk Engine, never by mood.
L72: Document as you build. A module without a completed QA checklist and a doc page is not “done.”

L281: 10.4 Implementation rules
L282: Point-in-time correctness: feature at bar t may only use data ≤ t (no lookahead). Every custom feature gets a lookahead unit test: shift the series by one bar, confirm feature values shift identically.

L284: No NaN leakage: warm-up rows (first ~252 bars) are marked, not silently dropped mid-pipeline; strategies decide whether to trade warm-up regions.
L285: Deterministic: same input → same output; no randomness anywhere in this module.

L310: 11.3 Miner-1: Brute-force combinatorial miner (build FIRST)
L311: Building blocks (each a versioned function returning a boolean series):
L312: Search strategy — staged, not full-cartesian (full cartesian of the above is ~10⁸; too much):
L313: Stage 1 — solo scan: every entry trigger alone × timeframes × pairs (~30 × 4 × 20 = 2,400 runs). Establish baseline expectancy of each trigger.
L314: Stage 2 — pair-up: top 30% triggers × every filter × fixed exit (~10 × 20 × 12 × 4 × 20 ≈ 192,000 runs — vectorbt handles this overnight).
L315: Stage 3 — exit search: top 500 combos × all exit models × exit params.
L316: Stage 4 — plateau analysis: for each survivor, perturb each parameter ±20%; score = fraction of neighbors still profitable (plateau score ≥ 0.6 required).
L317: Ranking score (in-sample, pre-validation):
L318: score = expectancy_bps × log10(n_trades) × plateau_score × consistency
L319: where consistency = fraction of profitable years in-sample
L320: 11.4 Miner-2: Genetic programming miner (Phase 9)
L321: Engine: DEAP (genetic algorithm framework) + gplearn-style expression trees.
L322: Genome: expression tree over feature primitives, e.g. AND(GT(RSI(14), 68), LT(close, MUL(VWAP, 0.98))).
L323: Fitness: OOS-segment-1 Sharpe × sqrt(n) − λ×tree_depth — penalize complexity explicitly (λ in config).
L324: Anti-overfit mechanics: (a) fitness evaluated on in-sample sub-segment A, selection pressure from sub-segment B (GeneTrader-style “selection bar”: the distribution of best-of-N luck); (b) max tree depth 6; (c) island model (separate populations, occasional migration) for diversity; (d) final candidates must pass the full Validation Lab like everything else — genetic search is a hypothesis generator, not a shortcut around validation.
L325: Compute budget: overnight runs (~8h); population 500 × 40 generations.
L326: 11.5 Miner-3: Local ML layer (Phase 10+, free)
L327: Task framing: predict P(trade profitable | features at entry) using the Trade Database itself as labeled training data — the system literally learns from its own edge mine.
L328: Models: gradient boosting (XGBoost/LightGBM) + logistic regression baseline. SHAP for interpretability (which features drive success — often reveals unknown session/regime structure).
L329: Validation: purged, embargoed time-series CV (López de Prado method) to prevent leakage; predictions only ever trade-sized through the Risk Engine, never naked.
L330: Hard rule: ML output is a filter/size adjuster on top of validated strategies, never a standalone black-box strategy, until you have 12+ months of live evidence.
L331: 11.6 Mining governance (what makes this professional)
L332: Every mining run is registered in runs/ with: config hash, git commit, date range, variant count, machine, duration.
L333: Trial budget accounting: the miner logs n_variants_tested — this number feeds the Deflated Sharpe calculation. You cannot compute honest significance without knowing how many things you tried.
L334: Survivors are candidates, never strategies. Only the Validation Lab can promote a candidate.
L335: Miner never sees post-2022 data. Enforced at the data-access layer.

L770: C2.6.1 The no-lookahead test pattern (use for every feature/block)
L771: def test_no_lookahead(feature_fn, sample_bars):
L772:     full = feature_fn(sample_bars)
L773:     for cut in [100, 500, len(sample_bars) - 10]:
L774:         partial = feature_fn(sample_bars.iloc[:cut])
L775:         pd.testing.assert_series_equal(
L776:             full.iloc[:cut - 1], partial.iloc[:cut - 1],
L777:             check_names=False)   # last partial bar may be incomplete
L778: Any failure = the feature peeks into the future = would have poisoned every backtest using it.
L779: C2.6.2 The synthetic-fixture pattern (use for every indicator)
L780: Build tiny series by hand where the answer is known:
L781: bars = make_bars(closes=[10, 11, 12, 13, 14])   # steady rise
L782: assert rsi(bars, n=2).iloc[-1] == pytest.approx(100.0, abs=1e-6)
L783: Five such fixtures per indicator beat 500 lines of “looks right” plotting.
L784: C2.6.3 The known-strategy parity pattern (engines)
L785: Buy-and-hold over 10 fixed windows with fees=0 must match qty × (exit − entry) to the cent. With fees=5bps it must be exactly 2 × notional × 0.0005 lower. Any engine that can’t reproduce arithmetic can’t be trusted with strategies.
L786: C2.6.4 The negative-control pattern (miner, edge engine, validation lab)
L787: rng = np.random.default_rng(42)
L788: noise_trades = pd.DataFrame({"pnl_r": rng.normal(0, 1, 5000), ...labels...})
L789: cards = scan_dimensions(noise_trades, dims=["hour_utc", "day_of_week"])
L790: assert (cards.p_adj < 0.05).mean() < 0.02   # false-positive rate under control
L791: Run after ANY change to statistics code. If random data produces “edges,” the lab is broken and every prior result is suspect.
L792: C2.6.5 The positive-control pattern
L793: Inject a known edge (e.g., +0.25R whenever day_of_week == 2) into noise. The edge engine must surface exactly day=2 in its top results; the validation lab must pass the synthetic strategy. If the system can’t find a planted edge, it won’t find real ones.
